## Picking one

For a new design, SHA-256 or BLAKE3 and move on. BLAKE3 when speed matters and you control both ends, SHA-256 when anything else has to agree with you.

For compatibility, the page of the thing you're matching wins. Ethereum wants [Keccak-256](/algorithms/keccak256), not SHA3-256, and the two differ in one padding byte. A Bitcoin address wants [HASH160](/algorithms/hash160) of the key's bytes, not of its hex. The XRP Ledger wants [SHA-512Half](/algorithms/sha512-half), which isn't SHA-512/256. Getting any of those almost right gives you a digest that looks exactly like the correct one.

For checksums somebody else published, whatever they published, MD5 included. For your own tables and dedup, [xxHash](/algorithms/xxhash) or [FNV-1a](/algorithms/fnv1a). Never for anything an attacker gets to choose.

For a password digest you need to reproduce, [scrypt](/algorithms/scrypt) or [PBKDF2](/algorithms/pbkdf2) with the exact salt and cost it was made with. [Salted KDFs](/guide/kdf) explains why the salt comes back. Keys from a secret that's already strong, a shared secret or a master key, come from [HKDF](/algorithms/hkdf) instead.
