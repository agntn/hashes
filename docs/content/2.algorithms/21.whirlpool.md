What do you get when an AES designer builds a hash? Whirlpool. Vincent Rijmen and Paulo Barreto published it in 2000 and named it after a galaxy, M51. Inside sits W, a cipher that looks a lot like AES pumped up to 512 bits. An 8x8 grid of bytes, ten rounds, S-box, shift, mix, add key. Each block keys W with the state, and the result goes back in with the block and the old state. That's the Miyaguchi-Preneel mode.

```ts
create("whirlpool").hash("abc").digest;
// "4e2448a4c6f486bb16b6562c73b4020bf3043e3a731bce721ae1b303d97e6d4c7181eebdb6c57e277d0e34957114cbd6c797fc9d95d8b582d225292076d4eef5"
create("whirlpool").hash("").digest;
// "19fa61d75522a4669b44e39c1d2e1726c530232130d407f89afee0964997f7a73e83be698b288febcf88e3e03c4f0757ea8964e59b63d93708b138cc42a66eb3"
```

Its S-box is small Lego. Three 4-bit boxes, E, its inverse and R, snap together into the 8-bit one. So this package ships 32 nibbles, not 256 bytes. The first block Whirlpool hashes builds the S-box, the eight lookup tables and the round constants. The constants are just rows of the S-box. Nothing up its sleeve.

## Which Whirlpool is this?

The final one. Whirlpool changed twice after 2000, and every revision prints 64 bytes:

| Name | What changed | The quick brown fox starts with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whirlpool-0 (2000) | the original, with a random S-box | `4f8f5cb5` |
| Whirlpool-T (2001) | the S-box built from 4-bit boxes | `3ccf8252` |
| Whirlpool (2003, this one) | the mix matrix, now cir(1, 1, 4, 1, 8, 5, 2, 9) | `b97de512` |

The sentence is "The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog". Three answers, and only the last is the one ISO/IEC 10118-3 calls Whirlpool. The 2003 fix was a single row of numbers. Put cir(1, 1, 3, 1, 5, 8, 9, 5) back and you're holding Whirlpool-T again. One row, a whole new hash.

So `create("whirlpool-t")` throws instead of guessing. `identifyDigest` computes Whirlpool for a 64-byte digest and names the two older ones as hashes it can't compute. A digest that matches nothing here might be one of them. PHP's `hash("whirlpool")` and RHash give the final one too.

## Is it broken?

No. Collisions on the hash reach 5.5 of its 10 rounds, and 8 on the compression function alone. A distinguisher covers all ten rounds of that compression function. Even the people attacking it call its impact on the hash limited. It sits in ISO/IEC 10118-3, and NESSIE picked it. VeraCrypt still offers it for deriving volume header keys.

Its digest is the whole state, though. So `Whirlpool(secret + message)` extends like SHA-256. Same 0x80, only the length field takes 32 bytes, not 8. [`extendDigest`](/guide/extend) forges it without the secret. Want a MAC? HMAC runs over its 64-byte blocks and matches PHP's `hash_hmac`.

It isn't fast. Expect a bit under half of SHA-512's speed here. Every 64 bytes cost ten AES-style rounds, twice. Got old Whirlpool digests to check? This is your hash. Starting fresh? SHA-512 or BLAKE2b.
